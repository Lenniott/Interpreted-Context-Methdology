# Polish Checklist

The line-level and structural checks to run in the polish stage. Work top to bottom. Pair with the hard constraints in `../../author-vault/voice-rules.md`.

## The Two Tests

- **Headline test:** read only the headlines. They should convey the whole story. If they do not, rewrite the headlines.
- **Five-minute test:** a busy reviewer should grasp the project, your role, and the outcome in a couple of minutes. If not, cut further.

## Structure

- The outcome is stated up front, before the process.
- The problem is clear within the first few lines and stated in one sentence.
- The strongest material is in the first half, not buried.
- Every section pulls weight. If a section does not change the reader's view of the work, cut or merge it.

## Thinking and Evidence

- At least one rejected path is shown, with the reasoning. This is the differentiator; do not skip it.
- Decisions appear with their reasoning and at least one real tradeoff.
- Every outcome claim is specific and sourced, or honestly hedged. No implied numbers.
- At least two impact-evidence types are present (see `../../shared/impact-evidence.md`).
- Where nothing was measured, the case study names what it would measure.
- The reader can tell exactly what the author did versus the team.

## Voice

- Zero violations of the hard constraints in voice-rules.md.
- No hype or marketing language. No obvious AI-generated filler.
- Sentences lead with the point. Specifics over abstractions.
- The opening hooks in one line. The closing reflection is honest and forward-looking.

## Scannability and Mechanics

- One idea per paragraph; key terms bold; enough white space.
- Visuals are curated, not a gallery. Each has a "why" caption.
- No em dashes. Use a comma, a period, or "--".
- Length is in range ({{TARGET_LENGTH}}).
- Title says what the case study is about, not just the project name.
