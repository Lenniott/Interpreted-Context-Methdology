# Draft Guide

How to write each section of a case study well. Pair this with the section structure in `../../shared/case-study-anatomy.md`, the impact toolkit in `../../shared/impact-evidence.md`, and the voice in `../../author-vault/voice-rules.md`.

## General Rules

- Lead with the answer. The outcome goes first, before the process.
- Write headlines that tell the story on their own. A reviewer reading only the headlines should understand the project.
- Show decisions, not just deliverables. A screenshot proves you made something; the reasoning proves you should be hired.
- Prefer the specific over the abstract. "Cut onboarding from nine steps to four" beats "streamlined onboarding."
- One idea per paragraph. Bold key terms. Use white space.
- Write in past tense, first person, owning your contribution while crediting the team honestly.

## Section by Section

### Headline and Outcome

A title that states the point, a one-line summary, your role, and the single strongest result. If you have a number, lead with it. If not, lead with a concrete qualitative change. This is the hook; a skimmer decides here whether to read on.

### Problem

State the real problem in your own words, in one sentence, and make it a question the reader wants answered. Then the constraints. Resist restating the brief; the gap between the brief and the real problem is often the most interesting part.

### Key Decisions

The longest section. Walk through the 2-3 decisions that mattered, in the order they mattered. For each: the options, the reasoning, the choice, and the tradeoff. Show at least one path you rejected and why. This is where REVEALS-THINKING and DEMONSTRATES-CRAFT are won.

### Evidence and Impact

Back the headline result with a chain of evidence from the impact toolkit: before/after, proxy signals, a quote, decision-influence. If nothing was measured, name what you would measure and why. Be honest about mixed results. Never imply numbers you do not have.

### Reflection

Two to four sentences. One honest thing you learned, framed forward. No tidy morals.

## Visual Evidence

Mark images inline as bracketed notes, for example `[before/after: the three states of the form]`. Pair each with a caption that explains why it mattered, not what it is. Curate key screens; a gallery of every screen is not a case study. Do not invent visuals that do not exist.

Aim for {{TARGET_LENGTH}}. If a skimmer cannot grasp the project from the headlines in a couple of minutes, cut further.
