# Angle Patterns

Common ways to frame a case study, and how to pick the one that fits. An angle is the single sentence that says what this case study is really about. The same project can support several; choose the one that serves your positioning and the reader.

## The Patterns

### The Hard Problem

The work centered on a genuinely difficult problem. The angle is the problem and the path you cut through it. Best when the thinking is the strongest material. Hits REVEALS-THINKING.

### The Turnaround

Something was failing and you changed its trajectory. The angle is the before-and-after and the decisions that moved it. Best when you have a clear outcome. Hits SHOWS-IMPACT.

### The Constraint

You did notable work inside a tight constraint (time, budget, legacy tech, a skeptical org). The angle is what the constraint forced and how you made it an advantage. Hits DEMONSTRATES-CRAFT and BUILDS-CREDIBILITY.

### The Alignment

The hard part was people, not the artifact. The angle is how you got a group to a decision. Best for collaboration-focused studies. Hits PROVES-COLLABORATION.

### The Craft Deep-Dive

The work itself was the achievement -- a level of polish, rigor, or technical depth worth showing. The angle is the craft and the choices behind it. Hits DEMONSTRATES-CRAFT.

## How to Choose

1. List the moments in the dossier with the most tension or the strongest evidence.
2. Match each to the pattern it best fits.
3. Pick the angle where your strongest material and your positioning line up. The best angle is the one only you could write, because of the specific decisions you made.
4. Reject angles the dossier cannot support with evidence.
