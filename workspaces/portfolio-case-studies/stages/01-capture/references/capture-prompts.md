# Capture Prompts

The questions that surface the facts a case study needs. Work through these conversationally in the capture stage. Skip what does not apply; chase what produces a strong answer. The aim is raw material rich enough to lead with an outcome, show real decisions, and prove impact.

## Intended Use

- Where will this case study live: a portfolio website, a specific job application, an interview presentation, or an internal showcase?
- If it is for a role, what domain does that role care about (mobile, enterprise, accessibility, research)?

## Context and Role

- What was the project, the product, and the team? When did it happen?
- What was your specific role? What did you personally own versus the team? Who did you collaborate with?

## The Problem

- What were you originally asked to do?
- Once you understood the situation, what was the real problem underneath the brief? Can you state it in one sentence?
- What constraints shaped what was possible (time, budget, tech, politics, users)?

## Key Decisions and Rejected Paths

- What were the 2-3 decisions that most shaped the outcome? For each, what options did you weigh and why did you choose the one you did?
- Which approaches or concepts did you consider and reject? Why? (This is the strongest differentiator -- capture it carefully.)
- Where did you make a tradeoff, and what did you trade away?

## Impact and Evidence

Walk the impact toolkit (`../../shared/impact-evidence.md`) and gather whatever exists:

- What was the strongest result? A number if you have one, a concrete change if you do not.
- Is there a before/after worth showing?
- Any proxy signals: adoption, task-success, fewer support tickets, faster handoff?
- Did research change a decision or kill a feature?
- Any user or stakeholder quotes? Who said them?
- If nothing was measured, what would you have measured, and why?

## Reflection

- What would you do differently now?
- What did this project teach you about your craft or how you work?
