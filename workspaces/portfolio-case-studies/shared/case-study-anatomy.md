## The Sections

A case study moves through five beats. Each beat answers one question for the reader.

### 1. Context

What was the situation? The product, the team, your role, the timeframe, and the constraints you were working inside. Set the stage in a few lines. The reader needs just enough to understand the decisions that follow.

### 2. Challenge

What was the real problem? Not the brief as handed to you, but the underlying tension once you understood it. State it sharply. A strong challenge creates a question the reader wants answered.

### 3. Approach

What did you do, and why? This is the heart of the case study. Walk through the key decisions, the options you weighed, and the reasoning behind the path you took. Show the thinking, not just the artifacts. Include the tradeoffs and the dead ends that mattered.

### 4. Outcome

What happened? The result, measured however the project allowed -- numbers, adoption, a shipped decision, a shift in how the team worked. Be specific and honest. If the outcome was mixed, say so.

### 5. Reflection

What did you learn, and what would you do differently? This is where the reader sees your judgment maturing. Short, honest, forward-looking.

## What Every Case Study Must Have

<!-- These are the non-negotiables. Your guidance notes likely have a stronger
     version of this list. Replace as needed. -->

- A clearly stated problem, framed in your own words
- At least one decision shown with its reasoning and tradeoffs
- A concrete outcome the reader can believe
- A visible sense of your specific role and contribution
- One honest reflection or limitation
