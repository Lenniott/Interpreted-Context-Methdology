# Impact Evidence Toolkit

How to prove a project mattered, especially when no hard metrics were tracked. Reviewers care that you think about measuring success, even when the company never did. The goal is a chain of evidence, not a single number.

Use at least two of the evidence types below in every case study. The capture stage gathers them, the angle stage decides which to feature, the draft stage writes them, and the polish stage verifies they hold.

---

## Reframe Intent First

If you cannot measure results, measure intent. Convert a vague goal into a directional hypothesis before anything else. "Make it better" is not strategic. "Increase feature adoption by making it discoverable earlier in the flow" is. State what you were trying to move and why.

---

## The Evidence Types

### 1. Before and After

Side-by-side visuals of the old and new state, each with a short problem -> approach -> outcome caption. The most direct way to show change.

### 2. Proxy and Directional Metrics

Early adoption trends, usability task-success rates, time-on-task, "support team reported fewer repeat questions," discoverability improvements. Directional signals are legitimate impact evidence.

### 3. User Insights That Drove Decisions

Show research that changed the roadmap or killed a feature. This proves you drove decisions, not just executed tasks.

### 4. Qualitative Quotes

A short, specific, attributed quote from a user or stakeholder, ideally paired with a photo or logo. Often more persuasive than a metric because the reader sees a real person benefit. Keep quotes to one or two sentences.

### 5. Efficiency and Collaboration Wins

Design-system adoption across teams, reduced design debt, faster design-to-dev handoff, a workshop that cut kickoff time. Internal wins count.

### 6. Scope and Effort as Proxy Numbers

Team size, number of assets, contract length, company size. When outcome numbers are missing, scope numbers show the weight of the work.

### 7. What You Would Measure

When nothing was tracked, name the metric you would have used and why. This signals business acumen even with zero analytics, and it is honest.

---

## Honesty Rule

Do not invent or imply numbers you do not have. A clearly framed qualitative outcome beats a vague or inflated metric. Not every project produces a clean number, and saying so builds more credibility than a flawless story. The point is to show you understand how to connect design to value.
